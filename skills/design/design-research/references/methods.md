# Methods

| Question | Method | Sample guidance | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| How do people do this today, and why? | Contextual interview, observation | Until themes repeat, per group | Self-report is not behavior [SRC-FLOW-021] |
| Can people complete the task with our design? | Moderated usability test | About 5 for one group; 3-4 per group for two; about 3 per group for three or more; several rounds [SRC-FLOW-001] [SRC-FLOW-002] | Not a measure of preference |
| How many succeed, how fast? | Quantitative usability test, analytics | 20+ [SRC-FLOW-002] | Needs a stable design |
| Which steps does the task really have? | Task analysis | A few skilled performers [SRC-FLOW-022] | Experts may skip steps they no longer notice (hypothesis) |
| Where does the end-to-end experience break? | Journey map | Built from research, not opinion [SRC-FLOW-004] | Needs a named goal |
| Which backstage processes cause it? | Service blueprint | Research plus staff interviews [SRC-FLOW-003] | When channels and hand-offs matter |
| What is obviously broken? | Heuristic review | Two or three reviewers | Finds candidates, not proof |
| How do competitors solve it? | Competitive review | Task-based, same tasks | Inspiration, not evidence |
| Does it work for disabled people? | Research with disabled participants and assistive tech | Include in every round | Automated checks do not replace it [SRC-TOOL-001] |
| Which option performs better? | A/B or multivariate experiment | Power analysis first | Ethics, novelty effects |

## Synthesis

1. Collect observations verbatim with participant ids (never invented).
2. Cluster into themes; count how many participants show each.
3. Separate observation ("4 of 5 did not find the export") from inference
   ("the label is unclear") and recommendation ("rename to Download").
4. Rate confidence by sample, method fit, and consistency.
5. Tie each theme to the decision question.

## Reporting limits

State sample, recruitment, method, what the study cannot show, and which
claims are self-reported. A finding from three people is a signal, not a
rate; never turn it into a percentage of the user base.
