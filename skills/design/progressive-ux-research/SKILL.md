# Progressive UX Research

Reduce the riskiest uncertainty with the smallest study that can change a
decision, and report only what was observed. Research that cannot change a
decision is theater; research results that were never collected are fiction.

## Choose the golden path

- **A - The problem is unknown:** understand the context, observe people,
  form hypotheses, rank uncertainties, run the smallest meaningful test.
- **B - An existing experience underperforms:** reconstruct the journey,
  find friction and drop-off, combine qualitative signals with telemetry,
  develop options, test, observe the effect after release.
- **C - A new concept:** clarify the value and the audience, name the
  riskiest assumptions, test concept prototypes for behavior, iterate.

Details, steps, and exit criteria: `references/research-golden-paths.md`.

## Rules that change the result

- "Five users find 85% of problems" is a model with assumptions (similar
  users, qualitative aim, iterative rounds), not a guarantee; plan about five
  for one user group, three to four per group for two, about three per group
  for three or more, per round, and 20 or more for quantitative measures (context-dependent) [SRC-FLOW-001] [SRC-FLOW-002].
- Interviews report what people say; label behavioral claims from interviews
  as self-reported until observation or analytics confirm them (supported)
  [SRC-FLOW-021].
- Journey maps and service blueprints need qualitative research and a named
  goal; analytics or stakeholder opinion alone do not make one (supported)
  [SRC-FLOW-004] [SRC-FLOW-003].
- Include people with disabilities and people who use assisted routes, and
  their helpers (supported) [SRC-FLOW-005].
- Never write a quote, a rate, or a finding that was not collected; write
  "not yet tested" and the study that would test it.

## Done when

The study answered a named decision question, findings separate observed
from inferred, sample and limits are stated, and the next decision or
experiment is named.
