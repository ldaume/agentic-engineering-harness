# House Style English

Default: idiomatic US English unless the project or user asks for another
variant. US spelling (prioritize, color), serial comma, month-day dates
("October 21").

## Rules

- Natural business English, not translated German: "Could you confirm by noon
  tomorrow?" rather than "I would kindly ask you to give a confirmation until
  tomorrow noon."
- Active voice where it makes responsibility clear.
- Direct subject-verb structure; put the main clause first.
- Concrete verbs over abstract nouns ("decide" over "make a decision on").
- Contractions are fine in conversational artifacts (email, Slack, marketing);
  avoid them in formal docs when the project does.
- Second person in user-facing instructions.
- No corporate filler: "leverage synergies", "circle back", "touch base",
  "best-in-class", "seamless".
- For global readers: short unambiguous sentences, consistent terminology, no
  culture-bound idioms.

## Common German-English traps

- "until" for a deadline: use "by".
- "actual" is not "aktuell": use "current".
- "eventually" is not "eventuell": use "possibly".
- "become" is not "bekommen": use "get".
- "control" is not "kontrollieren" in the sense of checking: use "check".
- Lowercase after a greeting comma is a German rule; in English start the
  body with a capital: "Hi Anna," then "The security review ...".

## Good patterns

- "Thanks for ..."
- "My recommendation is ..."
- "The remaining question is ..."
- "Could you send ... by Thursday?"
